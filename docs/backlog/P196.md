# P196 — The watch gate froze mid-video, and the freeze was permanent

**Raised by:** the client, 04.09.2026, with the network tab open — _"again the
same issue, i have watched the video completely, the next button is not
available for me to test the lernerfolgskontrolle and it says `50 % angesehen` /
this is the 10th time we are having this issue"_.

The tenth report is the important part. P153, P154, P158 and P168 each fixed a
real defect on this path and none of them fixed **this** one, because every one
of them was about what gets _accepted_ and this is about what happens after
something is refused.

---

## The evidence, from the client's own screenshot

```
contentId: "850da1ec-e2f4-4f9e-98f5-93772656766c",
accepted: 0,
rejected: [{segment: {startSec: 946.76106, endSec: …
seekCeilingSec: 479.761741,
status: "in_progress",
watchedPercent: 50,
watchedSegments: [{startSec: 0, endSec: 479.261741}]
```

A 15:52 (952 s) video, played to the end at **2×** — the player's own speed
control, visible in the screenshot. Credit stops dead at 479.26 s, the ceiling
with it, and a later report at 946.76 s is refused. `accepted: 0`.

## Two mechanisms, and neither survives alone

### P196-01 — one refusal was permanent

Reproduced before anything was changed:

```
flush 1: [0,30]   accepted 0, rejected faster_than_wallclock
flush 2: [30,45]  accepted 0, rejected beyond_ceiling
flush 3: [45,60]  accepted 0, rejected beyond_ceiling
…
credited after watching on: 0 %   stored []
```

`validateSegments` rejected a **whole segment** when it exceeded the wall-clock
budget, and a rejected segment does not advance `furthest`. So the ceiling
cannot advance because reports are refused, and reports are refused because they
begin past the ceiling. A deadlock, and there is nothing the learner can do
about it — including starting the video again, because the record keeps refusing
the far side of its own frozen edge.

That is the whole shape of the report: watched to the end, credit stuck
part-way, no way forward.

### P196-02 — 2× had no headroom to begin with

The budget was `elapsed × MAX_PLAYBACK_RATE + 2`. At the fastest rate the menu
offers, an honest claim **equals** the budget, so a flat two seconds was the
entire margin for error in a measurement taken between two database write times
against a media clock the browser advances on its own schedule.

Measured, over a full 952 s session:

| rate   | jitter ≤ 2 s on the measured interval |
| ------ | ------------------------------------- |
| 1.5×   | 100 %, never froze                    |
| 1.75×  | 100 %, never froze                    |
| **2×** | **46 %, froze at 447 s**              |

46 % against the client's reported 50 %, on the same shape. The two fastest of
the five rates the player offers were the two that could not be credited — §9.2,
for a control the product hands every learner.

## The fix

1. **Clamp, do not discard.** An over-budget segment is credited as far as the
   budget reaches and refused beyond it, and `furthest` advances to what was
   credited — so the ceiling tracks the learner instead of freezing. This is the
   same answer P158-01 already gave for the tail that overshoots the duration.
2. **`CLOCK_SLACK = 0.15`.** The budget becomes
   `elapsed × MAX_PLAYBACK_RATE × 1.15 + tolerance`. A proportional term rather
   than a bigger constant, for the reason P153-01 gave when it made the rate a
   multiplier: any flat number that absorbs jitter in a fifteen-second heartbeat
   is a number a slow reporter can hide a skip behind.
3. **`wallClockBudget` is the one home for the formula.** Three tests had
   `elapsed × 2 + 2` written out by hand and had to be rewritten when the bound
   moved; they now ask the function (§9.10b).

### What it costs, stated plainly

The effective cap is `MAX_PLAYBACK_RATE × 1.15` — a client may claim 2.3 media
seconds per real second instead of 2. That is the price of crediting 2× at all.
It is nowhere near enough to walk a video: a 952 s claim still needs 400 s of
elapsed time to be payable, and `watch.test.ts` pins both ends.

### The weakening I introduced and then removed

The first version funded the clamp from the whole budget, tolerance included.
`learning.service.test.ts`'s P55-01 case caught it: a client posting the whole
video **the instant it enrolled** has zero elapsed time, and a clamp paid for by
the flat tolerance handed it two seconds per request — unbounded, for a video
nobody opened. P55-01's exploit at a lower rate is not a fix.

The clamp is now funded by elapsed time alone. The tolerance forgives a claim a
shade over the line — such a claim is accepted whole and never reaches the clamp
— and buys nothing. That test passes unmodified.

## Watched red

- The two P196 cases fail on the old discard-whole behaviour.
- Removing `CLOCK_SLACK` fails three, including "lets a learner who was refused
  once carry on watching".
- Removing the `claimed` cap after a clamp fails the invariant at
  `expected 124 to be less than or equal to 62`.

That last one is a §9.1 finding in its own right. The invariant block first
passed with the cap removed, because no shape in it produced **two** clamps in
one batch — so the accounting that makes clamping safe was untested by the test
written to guard it. Two overlapping over-budget segments were added and it goes
red.

## What is not established

The reproduction matches the report in mechanism and magnitude — 46 % and frozen
against 50 % and frozen — but it is a **model of** the client's session, not the
session. I do not have that installation's request log. What would settle it is
two consecutive `progress` request/response pairs from the network tab during a
2× playback, which would show the measured interval directly.

Also not verified: that the browser journey exercises 2×. It does not today —
the fixture is an eight-second video at 1× — so this class remains invisible to
`pnpm test:e2e`. Named here rather than fixed, because a journey that watches a
long video at 2× costs minutes per deploy and that is a trade for the client.
