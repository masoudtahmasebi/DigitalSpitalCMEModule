# P54 — Two answers the platform would not give the person it holds them about

Raised 11.08 as decisions on questions flagged during QA Section 2. Both are
the same shape: the API knew something about the signed-in physician and had no
way to tell them.

---

## P54-01 · `/auth/participant/me` names the account · 0.5 h

**Status: done.**

The route answered `userId`, `customerId`, `role` — three values that mean
nothing to the person reading the screen. A physician who may hold accounts at
more than one customer, and who on the Keycloak plane may have been signed in
by WordPress without ever seeing a form on our side, had no way to answer
"which account is this".

It now also carries:

- **`email`** — the address they recognise. Optional, because a Keycloak realm
  need not release the claim; omitted rather than sent as `null`, since a null
  reads as "we hold none".
- **`subject`** — the identity provider's own `sub`. The only value that ties a
  session here to a user in the customer's realm, which is what a support
  conversation about a broken federated sign-in has to quote.

Both come off the validated principal. There is no parameter, so the route
cannot be asked about anybody else, and the existing cross-tenant case still
answers 401.

## P54-02 · A physician may read their own EFN · 2 h

**Status: done.** ADR-0004 amended — human review.

### What was wrong

The EFN was write-only, and the reasoning was sound as far as it went: it is a
physician's identifier at their Kammer, and an endpoint returning one is an
endpoint that can leak one.

What it left out is the person who typed it. The completion screen said _"Ihre
EFN ist hinterlegt."_ — true of every wrong EFN as well as every right one. A
physician who mistyped a digit months earlier, on another device, could read a
reassuring sentence about the wrong number, and the first sign of it would be
CME points credited to somebody else's account. ADR-0004 itself calls that the
worst failure available, **because it looks like success**.

The subject can compel the answer under GDPR Art. 15 regardless. The only
question was ever whether they get it from the product or from a mailbox.

### What changed

- **`GET /profile/efn`** → `{"efn": "…"}` or `{"efn": null}`.
- The Punktemeldung screen shows the stored number in full — a masked EFN
  cannot be checked, which is the only reason to show it — with **EFN
  korrigieren** beside it, going through `PUT /profile/efn` rather than
  submitting the whole form as a side effect of fixing a digit.
- ADR-0004 carries an amendment section; `docs/gdpr.md` §2 and the Auskunft
  section in §4 are corrected, and the EFN is no longer listed as an exception
  to "the learner can read everything we hold about them".

### What keeps it from being the leak the old rule feared

**The subject is the principal and can never be a parameter.** There is no
shape of this request that names another person, so there is nothing to
enumerate and no authorisation check whose failure would widen it.

A `customer_admin` holds a grant over a _tenant_; an EFN belongs to a
_physician_, who may hold enrolments at several customers. Those are different
scopes, and only the narrower one is opened — which is exactly what the
integration test asserts, by signing in an admin of the same tenant as the
learner whose EFN was just stored and expecting `null`.

That test was verified by breaking it: dropping the `WHERE user_id` from
`findEfn` — the "scoped to the tenant like every other console read" mistake —
turns it red and nothing else in the suite notices.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] `GET /profile/efn` returns the caller's own EFN
- [x] `null` before one is stored, asserted **before** the write so the case
      cannot pass against a hardcoded value
- [x] A `customer_admin` in the same tenant gets their own (`null`), not the
      learner's — verified red
- [x] No other endpoint returns an EFN; `efnPresent: boolean` unchanged on the
      admin surface
- [x] The screen shows it and can correct it; the correction does not complete
      the course
- [x] `CompletionScreen.test.tsx` exists — the screen that captures a
      physician's EFN, name and consent had no component test at all
- [x] ADR-0004 amended, `docs/gdpr.md` corrected, contract and SDK regenerated
- [ ] **Human review** — this reverses a documented data-protection decision.
      The reviewer's question is whether "the subject, and only the subject"
      is where the line belongs.

### Deliberately not done

**No audit entry for a self-read.** The audit log records administrative
actions on other people; a physician reading their own field is not one, and an
entry per page load would put the fact of the read in a table that is retained
longer than the read is interesting.

**No masking.** A masked EFN cannot be compared against an Arztausweis, which
is the entire purpose.
