# P199 — The time under the pointer, on the scrub bar

**Raised by:** the client, 07.09.2026 — _"please add the feature to see where in
the video the user is, by hovering over the progress bar of the video."_

---

## Why it earns its place

On a twenty-five-minute Fortbildung the bar is the only instrument for judging
distance, and a hover that reads nothing back leaves a person estimating from
pixels. It is also the control this platform asks the most of: a physician who
has to re-watch a passage has no other way to find it.

## What it does

The time under the pointer, above the bar, following it — and, where the gate
will not let the playhead go, saying so.

That second half is the part worth arguing for. The bar already draws the locked
remainder as a shaded region, but a shaded region is a thing you notice after
you have clicked into it and the playhead has snapped back. §9.2: a control that
reads back a time it will refuse to seek to looks like a control that works.

## The two things that keep it honest

1. **It shares the click path's arithmetic.** `seekFraction` and
   `seekPositionSec` — the same two functions, not a second reading of the
   geometry (§9.10b). A preview that disagreed with where a click lands would be
   worse than no preview, and `agrees with where a click actually lands` is the
   test that says so.
2. **It is `aria-hidden`.** The slider already announces its position through
   `aria-valuetext`, and this is the _pointer's_ position — a number a
   screen-reader user has no pointer to move. Exposing it would read a second
   answer to a question nobody asked.

## The trap in testing it

Every case in this block first passed against a label reading `0:00` at
`left: 0%`.

`fireEvent.pointerMove(el, { clientX })` delivers **`clientX: null`** here —
jsdom has no `PointerEvent` carrying coordinates, so testing-library falls back
to a plain `Event` and the init is dropped. A zero coordinate is
indistinguishable from a preview that works, so six green tests said nothing
(§9.1). Probed rather than assumed:

```
pointerMove -> [null]
```

The tests dispatch a `MouseEvent` typed `pointermove`, which carries `clientX`
and still reaches React's `onPointerMove`. The product keeps pointer events on
purpose: they are what make the readout work for a finger dragging the bar, not
just a mouse.

jsdom also gives every element a zero-sized `getBoundingClientRect`, so the
track is given one in the fixture — without it `seekFraction` divides by zero
and every case reads `0:00` again.
