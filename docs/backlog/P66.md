# P66 — Two credentials in one browser, and a rule its owner could not change

Both reported 12.08 from the deployed installation, both by clicking.

---

## P66-01 · Enrolment answered 403 whenever the console was open in another tab · 2 h

**Touches auth — human review.**

### What was wrong

`PUT /courses/{slug}/enrolment` answered **403** for a signed-in participant.
`GET /courses/{slug}` answered 200 on the same page load, with the same cookies.

The mechanism is invisible from either app, which is why it survived: **the
console and the portal call the same API host.** `verwaltung.digitalspital.com`
and `fortbildung.digitalspital.com` are different origins, but both talk to
`api.digitalspital.com`, and `ds_staff_session` belongs to _that_ host. So an
operator signed into the console sends their staff cookie on every request the
portal makes.

`authenticateStaffPlane` runs first. It saw its cookie, and for an unsafe method
it requires the staff CSRF header — which a participant app has no way to hold.
So it rejected. Only unsafe methods, because CSRF is checked on those alone:
reading a course worked, enrolling in it did not.

The guard's own comment already described the situation and drew the wrong
conclusion:

> _"A staff member's browser may carry both credentials — the console and the
> portal can share a parent domain — and the cookie is the one that names
> **them** rather than a learner."_

True for authentication. Wrong for a request the staff plane cannot
authenticate, where the correct reading is not "this is a staff member" but
"this is not a staff request".

### Why no test caught it

`asStaff` and `asLearner` send one cookie each. Every suite in the repository
sends exactly one credential per request — which is the one shape a real browser
does not produce. The bug lived in the combination, and nothing combined them.

### What changed

When the staff plane rejects for `csrf` **and the request carries a learner
credential** — a bearer token or the participant cookie — the guard defers to
the learner plane instead of throwing.

The protection is unchanged where it matters: a genuine staff request carries no
participant cookie, so a missing CSRF header is still a 403. That half has its
own test, because otherwise this fix would read as "omit the header to bypass
CSRF".

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] A participant enrols with a staff cookie also present
- [x] A staff mutation with no CSRF token is still refused
- [x] The test sends both cookies on one request, as a browser does
- [x] Verified red — removing the deferral fails on the reported symptom:
      `expected 403 to be 200`

## P66-02 · A super administrator could not remove their own second factor · 1 h

**Touches auth — human review.**

### What was wrong

`POST /admin/auth/second-factor` answered 403 for a super administrator.
`canRemoveOwnSecondFactor` refuses under a `required` policy, and ADR-0012 fixes
the **platform** policy — the one a super administrator falls under — at
`required`.

The Sicherheit screen offered the button anyway, so the most obvious control on
the page could only produce an error (§9.2). This is P38-07 repeating: that
entry recorded the same screen saying something true and leaving _the one person
able to change the rule_ with no idea they could.

### What changed

A super administrator is no longer bound by `required` for their own factor.
Everybody else still is.

### Why this is not a hole

A super administrator **owns the platform policy**. They could already move it
to `optional`, remove the factor, and move it back — three requests, all
audited, same outcome. The refusal never prevented anything; it made the outcome
undiscoverable.

The attacker question: does this help somebody holding a stolen
super-administrator session? No. That session can already relax the policy and
then remove the factor. The guard cost the legitimate owner their access and
cost an attacker two requests.

A `customer_admin` under a `required` customer policy still cannot remove their
own, and that is asserted — they do not own that policy, and for them the
refusal is the whole point.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] A super administrator may remove their own second factor under any policy
- [x] `customer_admin`, `department_admin` and `course_editor` stay bound by
      `required`
- [x] The rule is in `packages/domain` and exhaustively tested
