# P69 — "Entfernen" that removes nothing you can see

Reported 13.08, from `verwaltung.digitalspital.com`, build v0.1.206 · `a07c128`:

> _"i removed the 2factor for an account, and again after login, it is asking
> for setting a 2factor auth."_

Both halves of that sentence are true, and the product was behaving exactly as
designed. That is what makes it a defect: **the design was never on the screen.**

---

## What actually happens

`DELETE /admin/auth/second-factor` clears the enrolment. It does **not** change
the policy — and it must not, which is the whole point (below). So at the next
sign-in:

```
secondFactorStep("required", enrolled: false) → "must_enrol"
```

…and the operator lands on the QR code again. What they did was rotate their
second factor. What the button said was _entfernen_.

## Why the rule stays exactly as it is

A removal that also relaxed the policy would mean that **anybody holding a live
session could turn the second factor off permanently** — one stolen session, one
click, and a password is sufficient for that account for ever. The separation of
"clear the enrolment" from "change the rule" is the control, not an oversight.

The rotation is also the behaviour somebody actually needs: replacing a phone is
exactly "remove the old factor, enrol the new one at the next sign-in".

## So the fix is the screen, not the rule

Three changes on `Sicherheit`, all conditional on the policy this operator is
actually under — `applicableSecondFactorPolicy`, the API's own function, not a
second opinion computed in the console:

1. **Before the click.** Under `required`, a notice says the removal is a reset,
   that the next sign-in will ask for a new factor, and — for the person who
   wants none at all — that the Regel above has to change first.
2. **In the confirmation.** "Wirklich entfernen" becomes "Zurücksetzen und neu
   einrichten", so the last thing read before the irreversible click is what the
   click does.
3. **Afterwards.** A bare "Gespeichert." was shared with the policy form. A
   rotation now says so; a genuine removal under `optional` says _"Sie melden
   sich künftig nur mit Passwort an."_

## This is the third time on this control

- **P38-07** — the console drew "Zwei-Faktor zurücksetzen" on your own row,
  where the API refuses self-reset by design.
- **P66-02** — a super administrator could not remove their own factor at all;
  `canRemoveOwnSecondFactor` was widened so the button works.
- **P69-01** — the button works, and means something other than its label.

The shape is CLAUDE.md §9.2 and §9.4 in sequence: first a control that could
only fail, then a control that succeeds, then a control whose success is not
what the label promised. Making something work is not the same as making it
**say** what it does, and the second half is easy to consider finished when the
first half starts returning 204.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Under `required`, the screen says a removal is a rotation before the click
- [x] The confirmation names the act it is confirming
- [x] The message afterwards distinguishes a rotation from a removal
- [x] Under `optional`, none of that appears — there the label is already true
- [x] A `customer_admin` is judged by **their customer's** rule, not the
      platform's, via `applicableSecondFactorPolicy`
- [x] `Security.test.tsx` covers all five, and goes red when the condition is
      forced to `false`

## Deliberately not done

**A "second factor off for this account" control.** It exists already, one panel
up, as the policy — and it is scoped correctly: the platform's row belongs to a
super administrator, a customer's to that customer's administrators. Adding a
per-account override would be a second way to say the same thing, and the one
that is easiest to reach from a stolen session.

**Turning the platform policy off by default.** ADR-0012 fixes it at `required`
for accounts that belong to no customer, and that is the account with access to
every customer's data. A sole super administrator replacing a phone is served by
the rotation this ticket documents.
