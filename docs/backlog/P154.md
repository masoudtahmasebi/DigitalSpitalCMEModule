## P154-01 — the right-arrow key stepped exactly onto the seek ceiling

### The capture

From Masoud's DevTools, on a real session:

```
watchedSegments: [ {0 → 15.147841}, {20.147841 → 50.27983}, …
                   {60.613139 → 61.318741}, {66.318741 → 70.765732},
                   {75.233282 → 125.09301} ]
seekCeilingSec: 130.09301          (= 125.09301 + 5)
watchedPercent: 4
rejected: []
```

**Two gaps are exactly 5.000000 s** — `15.147841 → 20.147841` and
`61.318741 → 66.318741`. `SEEK_STEP_SEC` is 5. `seekCeiling` was
`maxWatchedPosition + 5`. So one press of the forward key landed exactly on the
ceiling; playback then carried the ceiling forward and the next press did it
again. A learner could walk an entire video in five-second hops, leaving five
unwatched seconds each time, while the player said _"Vorspulen ist nicht
möglich"_.

`rejected: []` also settles something: `faster_than_wallclock` did not fire on
that session, so **P153-01 is not the cause of these gaps.** Those seconds were
genuinely not watched and the warning was right.

### The comment that hid it

`seekCeiling` explained its tolerance as

> a small tolerance so that nudging the scrub bar forward by a frame at the live
> edge is not refused

A frame is about 0.04 s. The number was 5 — a hundred and twenty-five frames,
and exactly the forward key's step. §11 rule 9 again: a reason that the number
does not implement.

### The fix

`SEEK_CEILING_TOLERANCE_SEC = 0.5`, in `watch.ts` beside the rule that applies
it, and used as the default by both `isSeekAllowed` and `seekCeiling` so the two
cannot drift.

Half a second because what actually needs forgiving is one `timeupdate` sample
of jitter — browsers fire those about every 0.25 s — plus a nudge. It is the
same magnitude as `BOUNDARY_TOLERANCE_SEC` one function away.

**The property that matters is the ratio, not the number:** the tolerance must
stay below the smallest forward control the player offers, or that control is a
way to skip. `watch.test.ts` asserts exactly that against `SEEK_STEP_SEC` and
`SEEK_JUMP_SEC`, so raising it back to 5 fails a test rather than quietly
reopening the hole. Same shape as P153's speed-menu guard.

Rewinding is untouched and remains unbounded: coverage is a union, so
re-watching cannot inflate it.

### Four stale copies of the old tolerance this surfaced

`resume.test.ts` carried its own `toleranceSec = 5` in a helper and two
assertions built on it (`305`, `5`), and
`learning-flow.integration.test.ts:524` asserted `seekCeilingSec` as a literal
`605`. Four copies of one number, none of them the definition. A test helper
holding a private copy of a production constant is how a file keeps passing
while the thing it guards moves; the helper now defaults to
`SEEK_CEILING_TOLERANCE_SEC`, and the remaining literals carry the reason they
changed.

## P154-02 — a failed progress request discarded the intervals it carried

### The mechanism, from source

`WatchTracker.drain()` empties `#pending` unconditionally. `LessonScreen.flush`
drains, posts, and on anything that is not a 401 swallows the error — so the
intervals that request carried were **gone**. Nothing held them; nothing re-sent
them. The next flush carried only what had been watched since.

The catch's own comment said:

> a learner cannot act on it, the next flush retries, and the server recomputes
> the union from everything ever reported

The next flush does not retry: it carries newly observed intervals only. So a
physician watching through a connectivity blip lost exactly the seconds they
watched during it, permanently, under a screen reading _"Ihr Fortschritt wird
automatisch gespeichert"_.

### The fix

`WatchTracker.restore(segments)` puts unacknowledged intervals back at the front
of `#pending`; `flush` calls it on any failure that is not a 401. Restored
intervals are coalesced on the way out like everything else, and the server
merges and validates them against the wall clock — so handing an interval back
cannot credit anything that was not played. It only stops the client throwing
away its own evidence.

Not restored on a 401: that session cannot post again, the screen is about to
say so, and holding intervals in a tracker that is about to unmount only
pretends they are safe.

### And the overlap that made the retry meaningless

The 15-second timer did not wait for the previous request. On a slow connection
two flushes overlap and — because `drain` empties the buffer — the second
carries only what arrived in between, so the later answer can be computed from a
smaller union than the earlier. `flush` now serialises on an in-flight flag,
which is also what makes `restore` safe: intervals put back cannot be drained
again by a request already in flight.

## What this does not do

- **No durable queue across a reload.** Pending intervals live in the tracker
  and die with the screen. A durable queue is a storage and identity decision —
  scoping by participant, expiry, and never submitting one participant's
  progress under another — and it is not this ticket.
- **No back-fill.** Unions already stored keep their holes. Whether to re-credit
  affected enrolments is a compliance decision (§7).
- **The deep-link reload** (`#ds/inhalt/{id}` returning to the catalogue) is not
  touched here and remains unfixed.
