## P27-01 · Course detail: the fold, the rules, the duration column — **done** (#63)

- **Estimate:** 3 h

Three rows on page 02, and one of them was a bug rather than a difference.

**`Mehr lesen…` inline** (2.5, 3.2). The layout draws it at the end of the last
visible word, teal and bold. It was an underlined link on a line of its own,
under a `line-clamp`. `line-clamp` clips the box, so nothing can be placed after
the clipped text — and it clips _nothing_ when the text is already short, which
meant the toggle appeared on a two-line description and clicking it changed the
page in no way. That is the "control that looks implemented and does nothing"
this repository keeps finding in its own work.

So the cut moved into `read-more.ts`, where the component can ask whether
anything was hidden and draw the toggle only when something was. The cost is
stated in that file's header: collapsed, the tail is not in the DOM, so
find-in-page does not reach it. `aria-expanded` and one activation are what pay
for it.

`ReadMore` is one component for both places. It was two, and the design review
recorded the same defect against both.

**The Inhalte duration** (2.8). It read `25:24 Min. · 1 Kapitel`. The layout has
the duration alone, and on the MEDICE course — one chapter per module — the count
printed "· 1 Kapitel" on every row.

**Rules between sections** (2.9). On the sections rather than the container,
because which section is first depends on the data: a course with no description
starts at Lernziele, and `first:` follows the DOM.

Rows 2.1 (split hero) and 2.2 (orange meta icons) were already built by P19-03.
The review file said otherwise and was stale — which is its own lesson: a review
document nobody re-checks is a document that starts lying.

## P27-02 · Zertifizierung becomes informational — **done** (#60)

- **Estimate:** 4 h · **Review gate: human — assessment, certificates**

The tab was the module outline, plus the EFN field, plus **Fortbildung
abschließen**. Page 04 has none of that: it is five prose sections telling a
physician what the accreditation is, what they must do to earn it, how the points
are reported, and what the certificate will contain.

The difference is not cosmetic. A learner opened the tab to read about the
accreditation and met a form asking for their EFN — an identifier the platform
has no reason to hold until they have finished. Collecting it before the event
that justifies collecting it is the shape of problem `docs/gdpr.md` exists to
keep this platform out of.

Where each piece went:

| Was on the tab           | Now                                                                                           |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The EFN + name + consent | Its own screen (page 13), reached from the quiz-passed screen's **CME-Punkte geltend machen** |
| The module outline       | Deleted. The player's Modul Übersicht is the course's navigation                              |
| The certificate download | Under `Ihr Zertifikat`, which is the section that describes it                                |

**The outline's deletion has a consequence, and it is handled.**
`resumeContentId` is the first _incomplete_ content, so it is null once a learner
has finished — and the outline was the last way back in. A finished course now
falls back to its first content, so **Fortbildung fortsetzen** is never a dead
button. That is a navigation decision, not a compliance one; the server still
decides where an unfinished course resumes.

**Every threshold on the tab is the course's own.** A sentence promising 80 %
over a course configured at 70 would be this platform telling a physician the
wrong rule about their own CME points, so the test renders two differently
configured courses rather than one.

A course with no accreditation gets one sentence saying so — the client asked for
courses without points, and the seeded default customer is one.

## P27-03 · The player and the exam, pages 06 to 12 — **done** (#61)

- **Estimate:** 8 h · **Review gate: human — assessment**

### One shell, five screens

The layout draws the player, the exam's four states and the Punktemeldung
identically: teal region with one large rounded corner, the course title, an
orange **Zurück zur Übersicht**, and one white panel holding the screen on the
left and **Modul Übersicht** on the right.

They were two different things. The player had its own masthead and its own
sidebar; the quiz, the evaluation and the completion form rendered inside the
_course detail's_ tab panel — under a tab row the layout draws on none of those
pages, and beside no module list at all.

`CourseShell` is now all five. Beyond fidelity: the sidebar states are gate
verdicts, and a second copy built beside the exam would have been a second
reading of which chapter is unlocked.

It also fixed something the code had been claiming for two tickets. `StickyProgress`'
own comment said its whole reason for existing was "being the resume affordance
_while a video is playing_" — and it was rendered only inside the tab panel,
which the player returns before reaching. It was absent from the one screen it
was built for.

### The exam, one question at a time

Intro (08) → question (09–10) → passed (12) or failed (11).

**One submission, at the end.** The obvious way to build a paged quiz is to send
each page, and that would put partially scored attempts in the database, give a
learner a way to abandon mid-attempt and leave one there, and change what
"unanswered counts as wrong" means in `scoreQuiz`. The test asserts the call
count, not the copy.

**Back but not forward.** `Weiter` is disabled until the current question has an
answer — the layout's own arrangement, and it keeps the submission dense.

**`Mind. 8 von 11 richtig`** is `minimumCorrectAnswers` in `@ds/domain`. It
_searches_ `scoreQuiz`'s own question rather than deriving a number, so it cannot
round differently: `Math.ceil(total * threshold / 100)` agrees on 8 of 11 and
disagrees elsewhere, and "elsewhere" is every course that is not this one. The
property test walks every shape from 1 to 40 questions and 0 to 100 %.

**The scroll affordance** (9.3) is measured with a `ResizeObserver`, not guessed
from an option count — whether five options overflow depends on their length and
the widget's width, and a hint that appears when nothing is hidden is the same
class of lie as one that never appears.

**Pause becomes the exam** (6.6) on the **server's quiz gate**, not on a
percentage computed in the browser. The layout describes the swap as happening
"at 100 %"; a client deciding that for itself would offer the exam to a learner
the API is about to refuse, or would look right while the two disagreed about
what 100 % means (union coverage, not playhead).

### Deliberately not as drawn

- **`Abschlussprüfung starten`, not `Teilprüfung starten`** (8.4). The layout's
  button names a per-module assessment that exists nowhere in the thirteen pages
  and nowhere in this budget. S20 stays open; naming a feature that does not
  exist is worse than departing from the render.
- **The progress card sits above the video**, not inside the teal band (6.3).
  Every reading is there. The band is shared by five screens and the card is
  meaningful on one; putting it in the band would leave that slot empty four
  times out of five.
- **`63 % von 100 % angesehen`** stays on the course detail's progress card
  (2.10) although the layout does not draw it. The modules-completed sentence
  answers the wrong question for a learner who is stuck.

### Still open, and still not guessed

S20 (Teilprüfung) and S21 (EFN length: the layout says 18, the domain validates
15). Both are compliance semantics and `CLAUDE.md` §7 is explicit.
