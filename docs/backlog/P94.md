## P94-01 — the percentage was a fraction of the requirement, not of the video

### What was actually happening

The union **was** being checked. Measured on the build the client was looking
at, a fresh ten-second video reads:

| watched            | percent | gaps |
| ------------------ | ------- | ---- |
| 1 s                | 14 %    | 1–7  |
| 3 s                | 42 %    | 3–7  |
| a scrub to the end | 0 %     | 0–7  |
| 0–7                | 100 %   | —    |

So one second did not make it complete, and dragging the scrub bar still earned
nothing. What the client saw was the last second of a video they had already
covered to 0–6 — the union closing its final gap, which is the rule working.

**And the complaint is still right**, for a reason the arithmetic hides. P93-01
put the tail grace in the _denominator_, so the number was a fraction of the
**requirement** — seven seconds — while its label said _angesehen_ and the
learner could see ten. A number whose referent is invisible cannot be checked by
the person reading it, and the only available inference is the one that was
drawn: that it is not counting properly.

### The rule now

The percentage is a fraction of the **video**, always. The grace lives in one
branch instead:

> when there is nothing left for the learner to go and watch, the whole video is
> credited — including its last three seconds.

Same table, after:

| watched            | percent | gaps |
| ------------------ | ------- | ---- |
| 1 s                | 10 %    | 1–7  |
| 3 s                | 30 %    | 3–7  |
| a scrub to the end | 10 %    | 0–7  |
| 0–6                | 60 %    | 6–7  |
| 0–7                | 100 %   | —    |

Every figure is now something the client can check against the clock under the
player. The scrub earns its one real second and nothing more.

### The property that falls out, which is the point

```
watchedPercent(…) === 100   ⟺   uncoveredSpans(…) is empty
```

Two screens draw those — the percentage beside the section title and _"Diese
Stellen fehlen noch"_ under the player — and P68-02 was precisely the state
where one said finished and the other did not. The biconditional makes that
disagreement unrepresentable, and it is asserted over nine cases rather than
asserted once.

### The tolerance had to become a budget

`uncoveredSpans` forgives a gap below one playback sample, which was safe while
it only _listed_ things. Now that it decides completion, a per-gap tolerance
would be a way to skip content: a client reporting `[0,0.24] [0.49,0.73] …`
clears every gap individually and leaves half the video unwatched. It is summed
first, so at most a quarter second of the **whole file** can ever be forgiven,
whatever shape the holes are. There is a test that builds exactly that attack.

The list is also never emptied by its own display filter — a learner told
nothing is missing while the gate withholds completion is P85-01's report, and
is the one state this must not produce.

### Migrated, not deleted

The `+3` fixture durations from P93-01 (`903`, `103`, `203`) went back to the
honest `900`, `100`, `200`: the arithmetic no longer needs the denominator to be
round, because the denominator is the video. One old case changed meaning rather
than numbers — "never closes a hole in the middle, however small" now has a
one-second hole, and the tenth-of-a-second case beside it asserts the agreement
with the gaps list that P85-01 asked for.

## P94-02 — the colours, and the call to action that was not one

### The palette

`Player-Ansicht-*` draws a finished module as an **orange disc with a white
tick** and a module under way as an orange disc with pause bars. Ours were green
and teal — a generic status palette rather than this product's.

Orange is not decoration: `tailwind.preset.js` already says what the token means
— _"orange is resume the thing you started"_ — which is what a module in a list
you are working down is. It is also a **branded** variable, so a customer
restyling the widget restyles these; `status.completed` was a fixed green no
customer could reach.

### One distinction worth keeping

The layout gives the module you are inside a **pause** glyph and the chapter you
are on a **play** arrow. That is not a stylistic wobble: the module is a
container you are part-way through, the chapter is the thing in front of you.
`state.inProgress` — _"Wird bearbeitet"_ — is the module's; _"Wird angesehen"_
stays the chapter's. Announcing "Wird angesehen" on a module would be a claim
about five chapters at once.

### The sidebar is one panel, not five cards

Hairlines between rows, as drawn. Five bordered cards read as five separate
things rather than one outline of one course.

### The call to action

Two halves, and the second is the one that cost the client a wrong turn.

- **It was teal.** The reasoning on record was that the accent marks the course
  in progress and the exam is the way out of it. The client looked at a finished
  video and found no call to action, which settles it: the one thing a physician
  should do next is the one thing in the accent colour, whatever that thing is.
- **There were two controls for it.** `nextAvailableContent` returns whatever the
  server has open, and once a module's video is done that is the module's own
  Lernerfolgskontrolle — so the row under the video drew a second control for
  it, labelled with the exam's own title and nothing to say it _was_ an exam.
  That is the _"when i click on the title of lernerfolgskontrolle it goes to
  lernerfolgskontrolle, but the user does not know it"_ half, and it is §9.4.
  One exam, one control, and it is the orange one.

### The save icon

The layout puts a floppy before _"Ihr Fortschritt wird automatisch
gespeichert"_. Decorative — the sentence says the same thing in words — but it
is what makes a physician notice the reassurance at all.

Each of the three behaviours was watched to go red: the teal variant, the
duplicate control, and the module glyph.
