# P166 — The holes were a backgrounded tab, and the completion rule ignores text

**Raised by:** the client, 03.09.2026, from the deployed `4943541`, in six
reports. This ticket carries one fix and five findings, three of which correct
something stated earlier in the same session.

## P166-01 — a backgrounded tab is not a seek (fixed)

> _"i also let the video be played and i did my own stuff, and then i get this
> again: `Diese Stellen fehlen noch: 6:20–7:50`"_

The fourth report of missing segments on this project, and the first with a
stated cause: the tab was in the background.

`timeupdate` fires roughly four times a second in front. Backgrounded, the
browser throttles that timer hard **while the media element keeps playing** —
audio does not stop. So the next sample lands tens of seconds later at a
position tens of seconds on. `WatchTracker.observe` read any jump past
`SEEK_TOLERANCE_SEC` (2 s at 1×) as a seek, banked the interval, and opened a
new one after the hole. Ninety seconds is past `fillSamplingGaps`' limit of
`min(60, duration × 0.1)`, so nothing closed it.

The tracker's own header had named the cause and drawn the opposite conclusion:
_"a busy main thread, a background tab or a slow decode can stretch that to
seconds; treating a legitimately-played 1.5 s gap as a seek would lose real watch
time."_ The tolerance was raised for it. It was never enough, because a
throttled tab does not stretch the interval by a second — it stops sampling
entirely.

### The first attempt was wrong, and three tests said so

Crediting every forward jump made three existing tests fail, correctly. A scrub
from 0:05 to 5:00 is a forward jump too, and one of the three is explicitly an
abuse case. **The delta rule is a second line of defence behind `VideoPlayer`'s
`seeking` event and it is not mine to remove.**

### What separates them is time, not position

A media element cannot advance `currentTime` faster than wall-clock × rate, so N
media seconds of real playback take at least N/rate seconds of real time —
throttled or not. A scrub covers N media seconds in approximately none.

So a forward jump is bridged only when the clock agrees it could have been
played, at the fastest rate the player offers, with one sampling step of slack.
`observe` takes `nowMs` as an argument, defaulting to `Date.now()`, because the
whole property is about elapsed real time and a test that cannot control the
clock can only assert the position arithmetic that was already there.

This is the same shape as the server's `faster_than_wallclock` budget and
deliberately not a replacement for it: this side decides what to **report**, the
server decides what to **credit**, and it re-derives everything from its own
clock. A client that lies here still cannot get past that.

**Watched red both ways.** Restoring the old behaviour fails the throttling
case; removing the wall-clock bound fails four cases, three of them the
pre-existing anti-abuse ones.

## P166-02 — the EFN form on an unfinished course, and what actually happens

> _"i have two modules which are undone, the progress is 50 percent, and i can
> open …/punktemeldung and give my efn, so nothing checks if someone just give
> the url in frontend."_

**Two things I asserted while investigating this were wrong, and both are
corrected here rather than quietly.**

**The server does check.** `POST /courses/:slug/completion` recomputes the state
from stored rows and throws a 409 listing what is outstanding. It never trusts
the client.

**And the EFN is not kept on a refused request.** I suspected it was, because
`setEfn` runs _before_ the completeness check. Every request runs inside one
transaction (`db/tenant-db.ts`) and the throw rolls it back, so nothing is
persisted. I checked before writing it down; it is stated here because a
half-checked claim about personal data is worth correcting in public.

**And `CompletionScreen` already refuses.** It computes `blockers` from
`state.outstanding`, renders _"Es fehlt noch: …"_, and disables the button —
P82-01 did exactly this.

### So why did it let the client through?

Because the course **was** complete, and the screen was right.

`isCourseComplete` is watch coverage plus quizzes. `courseWatchCoverage` counts
`kind === "video"` only. A module of two text sections, one video and one exam:
watch the video, pass the exam, and the course is complete with both text
sections untouched. The rollup that renders "50 %" counts every compliance
content — `isComplianceContent` excludes only `material` — so text is in the
**denominator of the number on screen** and in **neither side of the gate**.

That is one defect wearing two faces, and it is the whole of this report and the
next.

## P166-03 — a text section can never be finished

> _"in one module i have only text, when i click on `Weiter: Test Title` it does
> not show this as done, the same also for the next one"_

The same finding from the learner's side. `POST /contents/:id/progress` accepts
`kind === "video"` and nothing else, so a text or details section has no
completion event at all — it cannot be ticked, and it cannot hold a course back.
This is P87-08, filed and never actioned.

**This is a compliance-semantics question and §7 forbids guessing it.** Two
defensible rules:

1. **Text counts.** A section of the accredited Fortbildung is a section; the
   physician must open it, and the course waits. Needs a completion event, a new
   `outstanding` condition, and a decision about enrolments already completed
   under the old rule.
2. **Text does not count**, in which case it must leave the displayed
   percentage too — a course that reads 50 % and is complete is the defect, and
   removing text from the rollup fixes it without changing what is required.

The difference is whether a physician can be issued CME points without opening a
section the Bescheid lists. **That is the client's to answer**, and it is in
`docs/show-stoppers.md`.

## P166-04 — reaching the end of a video should credit it

> _"the finish of the video signals that the whole was watched, for example this
> comes in handy for the videos that are under 12 seconds."_

Half of this already holds: `creditedDurationSec` returns the whole length for a
video at or under `TAIL_GRACE_SEC`, so a short video's _denominator_ is not the
problem. What is asked for is the _numerator_ — `ended` as evidence the whole
thing played.

Not done, and it needs care rather than a flag: `ended` is a client claim like
any other, and the server must not credit a video because a POST said so. The
defensible form is the one already in use for the tail — credit the remainder
when the reported union covers all but a bounded gap — which is `fillSamplingGaps`
with `ended` as the trigger rather than a new trust. Specified, not written.

## P166-05 — a finished course looks the same in the catalogue

> _"in the list view for the courses that i have finished, there is no
> difference in the view."_

Partly already there: the catalogue renders _"Abgeschlossen – Zertifizierung noch
offen"_ under a completed course, which is visible in the client's own
screenshot. What has no marker is a course that is **fully** finished —
certificate issued — which reads identically to one never started apart from the
button label. Not done.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] A backgrounded tab does not cut a hole in the watched union
- [x] A forward scrub still cannot claim the span it skipped
- [ ] Text sections: rule decided by the client, then implemented
- [ ] `ended` credits the remainder of a video
- [ ] A certified course is distinguishable in the catalogue

## Not done

Everything above except P166-01, and the reason is §7 for P166-03 and honest
scope for the rest. P166-01 was fixed because its rule needed no decision: a
backgrounded tab is not a seek under any reading.

**None of this is tested in a browser.** `apps/e2e` needs a Docker daemon this
sandbox does not have. The throttling case in particular is a unit test of a
clock the real browser controls.
