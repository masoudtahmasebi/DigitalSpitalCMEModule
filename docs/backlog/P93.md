## P93-01 — the tail grace

### What the number 92 was

Ten seconds of video, watched to the end, credited 9.2 s. The last `timeupdate`
before `ended` lands a fraction short, and 9.2/10 floors to 92.

`BOUNDARY_TOLERANCE_SEC` — half a second, added in P29-01 for exactly this shape
— was not enough, because it assumes the sampling interval is the only error.
There are at least three, and the other two are much larger:

1. **The sampling interval.** `timeupdate` fires about every 250 ms.
2. **A stored length longer than the file.** `durationSec` is written from the
   object's own header; a container that rounds up, or a re-encode after the
   length was stored, leaves a tail nobody can ever reach. That is **P87-07**,
   still open, and it is the case that pins the percentage at one number however
   many times the learner re-watches.
3. **The flush.** A segment is closed at the last observed position, not at
   `duration`, so a browser that stops firing events early reports short
   whatever the person did.

### The rule

`TAIL_GRACE_SEC = 3`, and one function — `creditedDurationSec(durationSec)` —
that every rule dividing by a length now goes through:

| Rule                   | Before                 | Now                                        |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| `watchedSecondsWithin` | capped at `duration`   | capped at the credited length              |
| `watchedPercent`       | `/ duration`           | `/ credited`                               |
| `uncoveredSpans`       | walks to `duration`    | walks to `credited`                        |
| `courseWatchCoverage`  | `totalSec += duration` | `+= creditedDurationSec(…)`                |
| `watchedCoverageBars`  | (new)                  | fills the tail when nothing is outstanding |

### What it does **not** weaken

The union is still the union (§4 invariant 5). What changed is the **length
being measured against**, not the rule measuring — every second before the tail
must still be genuinely covered, so dragging the scrub bar to the end still
earns nothing. `watchedPercent([{0,10}], 10)` on a scrub to the end is 0.

A video shorter than the grace keeps its whole length. Shrinking a two-second
clip to zero would credit somebody who watched none of it, which is the one
outcome worse than the bug being fixed.

On MEDICE's accredited course three seconds is 0.2 % of a twenty-five-minute
module, against a rule the Anerkennungsbescheid states as a proportion of the
material. On a ten-second test upload it is 30 %, which is the case that
produced the report and is not accredited content.

### Why the bar had to change too

`coverageBars`' own header claims the property _"the bar and the number cannot
tell different stories"_ — and the grace breaks it for the watched track alone:
"100 % angesehen" over a bar with an unfilled sliver. Two pixels on a real
module; a third of the bar on the client's ten-second upload, which is the
screen in the report. `watchedCoverageBars` fills the tail **only when
`uncoveredSpans` is empty**, so a hole in the middle is never closed by it.
`bufferedBars` deliberately does not go through it: buffering is a fact about
bytes, not a requirement anybody has met.

### Where the rule is enforced, and what would notice if it stopped

- `packages/domain` — the boundary at the second: complete at 9.2 s of 10,
  complete the moment the tail begins (7 s), **not** complete at 6 s, still 0 %
  for a scrub to the end, and a hole in the middle still a hole. Removing
  `TAIL_GRACE_SEC` turns 18 of 728 red.
- `apps/api` — §9.7, name the caller: `learning.service.test.ts` asserts 597 s
  of a 600 s video completes on a course requiring 100 %, with 596 s as the
  control. The domain suite would be green with the service dividing by the raw
  length; this one goes red.
- `learning-flow.integration.test.ts` now reads 19 % where it read 20 — 117 of
  597 rather than 120 of 600 — so the credited length is pinned through the
  HTTP boundary as well.

### Sixteen stale tests, migrated rather than deleted

Every one encoded a property that still holds against the credited length, so
each was moved onto it rather than removed. Where the point was a percentage
boundary the fixture durations gained three seconds (`903`/`103`, `oneVideo` at
8 with the case rewritten), so the figures still read as percentages of the
requirement and the arithmetic stays legible.

Two changed shape rather than numbers:

- _"stops at half a second — a second short is a second short"_ kept only its
  **start** half. The far end no longer has a half-second answer, and saying so
  in the test name is better than an assertion that quietly tests something else.
- _"names a gap at the very end"_ now names one **up to where the requirement
  ends**, and gained a sibling asserting that the tail itself is never named —
  which is the `0:09–0:10` in the report.

### Not exported

`creditedDurationSec` and `TAIL_GRACE_SEC` are not in `packages/domain/src/index.ts`.
Everything that must know about them applies them inside the package; exporting
them would put a second route to the same arithmetic one import away from an API
or a screen, which is how P68-02 happened. `scripts/unused-rules.mjs` reported
both, and this is the answer rather than a suppression.

## P93-02 — the journey fixture, assessed and left alone

`kurzvideo.webm` is eighteen seconds, so its gate now opens at fifteen: a sixth
of the fixture is grace, against 0.2 % of a real module.

Not regenerated longer. A longer file costs wall-clock time on every run and
buys no coverage — the boundary is pinned to the second by the domain and API
suites above, and what the journey proves is that a physician who watches the
video gets through, which it still does by playing all eighteen seconds on the
clock. Written into `journey.spec.ts` and `fixtures/README.md` so the ratio is a
recorded decision rather than a thing nobody noticed.
