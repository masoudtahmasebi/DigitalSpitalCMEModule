# P58 — What the QA pass through EIV-FOBI found

Raised 11.08 by the QA agent's Section 7, run against the `@ds/eiv-client` mock
on `127.0.0.1` — never a live Ärztekammer endpoint. The submission path,
credential handling, retry policy and window enforcement all behaved. Two
deadline **records** did not match the deadline the platform actually enforces.

Both are the same shape: a computed rule and a stored copy of its answer, and
nothing keeping them honest.

---

## P58-01 · The correction window was never recorded · 0.5 h

**Status: done.** Touches EIV — human review.

### What was wrong

`eiv_submissions.correction_window_ends_at` was NULL on every successfully
submitted row, permanently.

The window opens when the first Meldung lands, so on a first attempt the plan
computed _before_ that submission has no window to report — and the service
stored exactly that `undefined`. A submitted row is never swept again, so
nothing ever came back to fill it in.

QA saw it directly: `first_submitted_at` set, `correction_window_ends_at` null,
on a row that had just been accepted by the mock.

The consequence was quiet, which is why it survived. Nothing _decided_ from the
column — the domain recomputes the window from `first_submitted_at` whenever it
is asked. But the column is the record an operator reads, an export carries and
a support query answers from, and NULL there says "no correction window" about
a Meldung whose window closes in seven days. CLAUDE.md §9.6 names that shape:
an all-null answer indistinguishable from "unset".

### What changed

The service recomputes the deadlines with the `firstSubmittedAt` it is about to
record, and stores that. One rule, `eivDeadlines`, asked with the right inputs.

The window is anchored to the **first** submission and stays there: a row that
retried on day three does not get ten days to correct. That is now its own test,
because the obvious implementation — "seven days from this attempt" — passes
every other case in the file.

### The test that was named for the behaviour and asserted the opposite

`eiv.service.test.ts` had a case called _"stamps the correction window from the
first submission"_ whose assertion was `toBeUndefined()`. It passed, and it was
the defect written down. This is the second such case this QA cycle (P56-01 was
the first), which is worth naming as a pattern: **a test whose name and
assertion disagree is a defect that has been observed and accepted.**

## P58-02 · The alerter and the submitter disagreed about a statutory deadline · 1.5 h

**Status: done.** Touches EIV — human review.

### What was wrong

Two answers to "when is this Punktemeldung due":

- the **submission sweep** recomputes it from `event_end_at` through
  `eivDeadlines` — the rule;
- the **deadline alerter** read the `report_due_at` column.

QA made them disagree in one sweep. With `report_due_at` moved to yesterday and
`event_end_at` still today, the alerter raised `level: "overdue",
hoursRemaining: -24` while the submitter — correctly — submitted the row. One
of the two was wrong about a statutory date and nothing in the system could
have said which.

They agree in normal operation, because both columns are written once at queue
time and never updated. That is exactly what makes it worth fixing before
launch rather than after: the disagreement appears the first time anybody
corrects a completion date, and the symptom is either an alarm nobody can act on
or silence while an 8-day window closes. CLAUDE.md §4 invariant 6 — one rollup
path — is the same rule, one table across.

### What changed

`unreported_eiv_submissions` returns the **inputs** — `event_end_at` and
`first_submitted_at` — instead of a precomputed deadline (migration 0040), and
the alert service applies `eivDeadlines`. The lookback filter moves to
`event_end_at` too, widened by the reporting window, so a row cannot be excluded
from the alert sweep by the very column that is wrong.

`report_due_at` stays on the table. It is a useful record of what was computed;
it is simply not what anything decides from.

### Two things this ticket got wrong first, both caught

**The migration reinstated a predicate that P33-01 had already fixed.** The
first draft copied migration 0011's `status <> 'submitted'` — the definition a
reader finds first — and silently reverted 0035's allow-list, which exists so a
deliberately withdrawn Meldung does not alarm three times about a decision a
named human made. The integration suite failed on it. CLAUDE.md §9.9's
corollary, one layer in: **the oldest definition of a thing is not its current
one.**

**The definer role's grant was too narrow, and said so.** `ds_binding_resolver`
holds column-level SELECT and nothing wider, so reading two new columns failed
with `permission denied for table eiv_submissions` rather than quietly
succeeding. The grant is now exactly those two columns.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] The alerter derives its deadline from the event, through the same
      function the sweep uses
- [x] A row whose stored deadline disagrees cannot even be expressed by the
      fixture any more — `PendingSubmission` carries the event, not a deadline
- [x] The withdrawn-Meldung allow-list survives the function replacement
- [x] The definer role gains exactly the two columns it now reads
- [x] Verified red at both layers: reverting the correction-window recompute
      turns one unit case red; replacing the alerter's `eivDeadlines` call with
      a flat eight-day offset turns the escalation integration case red
- [x] `pnpm verify` green; 476 integration tests green
- [ ] **Human review** — EIV. The reviewer's question is **whether the derived
      deadline is authoritative and the stored column merely a record**, and —
      for P58-01 — **whether the 7-day correction window is anchored to the
      first Meldung or to the most recent attempt.** The Bescheid says seven
      days and does not say seven days from what. See
      `docs/review/compliance-review-packet.md` §3 and §4 for what goes wrong
      either way.

## What Section 7 confirmed as already correct

Run against the mock at `127.0.0.1:4011`, with the live guard in place: the
worker logged _"EIV worker sweeping every 5s against http://127.0.0.1:4011"_
and refuses any non-local endpoint without `EIV_ALLOW_LIVE`.

- **7.1 — the VNR is readable, the password is not.** `GET /admin/courses/{slug}`
  returns `vnr` and `hasVnrPassword: true`; the only key matching /password/ in
  the whole response is that boolean.
- **7.2 — a completion queues a Meldung**, once per enrolment (a unique
  constraint enforces it), with the VNR, the EFN and `rolle: TEILNEHMER`.
- **7.3 — the worker did the whole thing.** HTTP Basic against
  `/fobi/veranstalter-auth/jwt` → a token → `POST
/fobi/veranstalter/push_teilnahme` with the EFN, both Punkte flags and the
  Teilnahmedatum; row `submitted`, `first_submitted_at` set; `audit_log` carries
  `eiv.submitted` with `attemptCount` and **no EFN, no password, no payload**.
- **7.4 — the credential is unreachable.** Absent from the learner's course
  read, from `GET /admin/courses`, from the course detail and from
  `/eiv/event`; the learner's own attempt at the admin route is 403. The column
  is 44 bytes of AES-GCM ciphertext and does not contain the plaintext.
- **7.5 — an expired window is not submitted.** With the event nine days old the
  sweep marked the row `window_closed`, wrote `last_error:
reporting_window_missed`, audited `eiv.abandoned` with `windowClosed: true`,
  and made **zero calls** to EIV. (QA's name for the state was
  `deadline_missed`; ours is `window_closed`.)
- **7.6 — a 500 retries and then succeeds.** `failed_retryable`, `last_error:
"server"` — the failure _kind_, never a payload — `next_attempt_at` ten
  minutes out; on retry, `submitted` at attempt 2. Both attempts audited.
- **7.7 — the alert mechanism exists and fired during this section.**
  `eiv.deadline_alert` in the audit log, an `error`-level log line always, and a
  webhook POST when `ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL` is set. Four levels: `warning` at 48 h,
  `urgent` at 12 h, `overdue` once the window has passed, and `blocked` the
  moment the worker gives up, whatever the clock says. The alert carries the
  enrolment id, the level and the hours remaining — no EFN, no name, no course
  title, because the destination is a chat room with an unknown membership.

### A note on the deadline arithmetic

Both deadlines land at the **end of a German calendar day**, not at an exact
instant eight or seven × 24 h later: a Meldung is due by the end of the eighth
day, and Berlin is where the Kammer is. So a submission at 22:30 UTC on the 11th
— 00:30 on the 12th in Berlin — has a correction window ending 21:59:59.999Z on
the 19th. That is the rule, not an off-by-one.
