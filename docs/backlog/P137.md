# P137 — Section 2 of the QA self-audit: progress integrity

**Status:** complete. All three invariants hold. Two were enforced and unasserted;
they are asserted now, and both new tests were watched to go red.

---

## Invariant A — the union, not the furthest position

**2.1 was already covered**, by name:
`counts the union of watched intervals, not the furthest position`. Watching
0–60 and 540–600 of a 600-second video credits 20 %, and the test's own comment
says what it is guarding: _"a max-position implementation would call this 100 %"_.

**2.2 was not**, and it is not the same case. The union test reaches its
conclusion from two intervals; the audit's second case is a **single fragment at
the very end, as the first update against an untouched video** — what a client
sends when somebody drags the scrub bar to 09:55 and lets it run. "Furthest point
reached" and "sum of intervals" agree on a learner who watches from the start and
diverge completely here, so a naive implementation passes 2.1 and fails 2.2.

`credits a drag to the end as the seconds it claims, not as the position` now
covers it: five seconds of six hundred, which floors to **0**, not 99.

The server _accepts_ the segment, and that is deliberate. Five seconds of
playback is plausible and nothing needs to refuse the report; what must not
happen is the report being worth more than the seconds in it.

## Invariant B — the gate and the reported figure cannot diverge

**2.4 needs no test, and stating why is the answer.** They are not two formulas
that agree; they are one local variable:

```ts
const percent = watchedPercent(merged, content.durationSec);
const status  = percent >= required ? "completed" : percent > 0 ? …;
await this.repository.upsertProgress({ …, status, watchedPercent: percent, … });
```

The gate and the number the learner and the admin see are computed once and used
twice. There is exactly one call to `watchedPercent` in the whole API.

## 2.3 — one rollup path

`summariseEnrolment` is the single function, and both planes call it:
`learning.service.ts` for the learner's own screen and `admin.service.ts` for the
reporting table. It calls `rollupProgress` once over records from the same shape.
`completion-flow.integration.test.ts` already asserts the outcome — _"reports the
participant's figures exactly as the learner's own screen does"_ — which is the
§4 invariant 6 property tested rather than assumed.

## Invariant C — the client cannot name its own percentage

**2.5 was structurally true and unasserted.** `progressReportSchema` accepts
`{ segments, lastPositionSec? }` and zod strips what it does not name, so a
client-sent `watchedPercent` never reaches the service.

That is exactly why it is now a test. The property is one `.passthrough()` or one
convenience field away from being untrue, and nothing else in the suite would
notice, because every other case sends a well-formed body.
`ignores a watchedPercent the client sends for itself` posts
`{ segments: [0–5], watchedPercent: 100, status: "completed" }` and asserts the
server's own small number comes back.

## Watched to go red

Two sabotages, and the second is the one that shows the tests are not
interchangeable.

**Max position instead of the union** — three failures, including the new case
reporting exactly the number the audit warns about:

```
× credits a drag to the end as the seconds it claims, not as the position
    AssertionError: expected 100 to be +0
× ignores a watchedPercent the client sends for itself
× counts the union of watched intervals, not the furthest position
```

**Trusting a client-sent `watchedPercent`** (a field added to the schema and
`report.watchedPercent ?? …` in the service) — **one** failure, the invariant-C
test alone:

```
× ignores a watchedPercent the client sends for itself
    AssertionError: expected 100 to be less than 5
```

Each invariant has a test that fails for its own reason and not for the others'.
